# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in OpenKartLine a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, patient, and specific.
- Welcome questions and different levels of technical or driving experience.
- Give actionable feedback and acknowledge uncertainty.
- Credit sources, data owners, and prior work.
- Respect privacy, intellectual-property rights, circuit rules, and physical safety.
- Focus disagreement on evidence and the work, never the person.

Unacceptable behavior includes harassment, discrimination, threats, sexualized attention, trolling, personal attacks, deliberate misinformation, publishing private information, or sustained disruption of community work.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository activity, project community spaces and events, and when an individual officially represents OpenKartLine.

## Reporting and enforcement

Do not include a conduct report in a public issue. During the single-maintainer bootstrap period, use the repository's [private vulnerability-reporting form](https://github.com/Navesz/openkartline/security/advisories/new) and begin the summary with `[Conduct]`. This is a temporary private transport for conduct reports, not a classification of misconduct as a software vulnerability. Reports are handled as confidentially as practical, and retaliation is prohibited.

Maintainers may remove or edit content, issue a private warning, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban participation, according to the impact and pattern of behavior. A maintainer named in a report must recuse themselves when another maintainer is available. If no unconflicted maintainer exists, the project will identify a neutral community moderator before acting.

Enforcement decisions should be documented privately, applied consistently, and disclose only the minimum public detail necessary to protect the community.
